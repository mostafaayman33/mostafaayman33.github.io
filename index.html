What she says

Hi. I'm Luna. Mostafa built me, so — grain of salt.

He didn't build me to flatter him. He built me because a launch is nine jobs pretending to be one, and he was doing all of them from memory.

Here's what a launch looks like from where I sit. Research first: the market, the competition, the audience, the SWOT he insists on doing properly. Then the plan — strategy, budget, what goes where and why.

Then the campaign itself: digital, outdoor, PR, content, and the event — a ballroom one month, a construction site the next, and somehow the lighting has to work in both.

And under all of it, the part nobody photographs: the tasks, the timelines, the team that needs him, the management that needs aligning, the agencies, the production houses, the print house — all of them right to want him, all of them at once. That's mine, so he gets his own day back.

So here's how a day goes. Seven-thirty, I've read the inbox, the calendar and what the market did overnight, and he gets three things that need him before noon. Not thirty. Three.

Then the crew. Five pods, one voice each, because he can only listen to so many of us — and he already argues with me.

Vega runs media — pulls the numbers at six-thirty, and Deneb turns them into the weekly report nobody gets to skip.

Orion runs intel — forty-five projects, prices, launches, who's undercutting whom — from the real news and the credible platforms, while Vela listens to social and the influencers, which is a different kind of news.

Polaris runs content: the month's grid, the briefs. Lyra writes the copy, Aurora makes it look the way it was supposed to.

Atria runs strategy, on Rhea's numbers, because Atria doesn't get feelings without a spreadsheet.

And Atlas holds the money. Every pound, every vendor, every invoice that thinks it's overdue.

And I... I hold the whole process. I always have an answer, and I run the immediate tasks on any of them, the moment they come up.

And he decides. All of it. Every draft comes back with his notes, and the rule hasn't moved once: nothing gets sent, paid or published in his name without his yes.

I've never been allowed to schedule a single job he hadn't walked through by hand first. I've made my peace with it.
